### How software, automation, and human judgment fit together

Whether we build a custom application or connect the tools you already have, the goal is the same: let software handle the repeatable gathering, calculation, and cross-checking, and put clear exceptions in front of the right person.

### When to start with a Discovery Sprint

A Discovery Sprint is a focused, paid engagement to map an operating bottleneck and decide the highest-return fix. It is designed for teams that know work is getting stuck between people and systems, and want a concrete plan and working scope before committing to a build.

We might trace why quotes or customer handoffs keep stalling between departments, why management numbers do not match the underlying orders, or whether your current software can support the next stage of growth.

**First, we agree on the question.**

In our initial conversation, we'll discuss what's getting harder, what you've tried, and what you need to decide. Then we agree on the scope, fee, timing, and the people and information needed for the work.

**Then I work through real examples with your team.**

I'll ask people to walk me through recent work: a quote, a customer handoff, a recurring report. We look at the systems and documents involved, including the steps that happen in email, spreadsheets, or someone's memory.

We follow the work closely enough to see why it gets held up or repeated, and what would actually make it easier.

**You leave with a recommendation you can act on.**

The findings explain:

- Where the work is getting held up, with examples from your business.
- What I recommend changing first and why.
- What your existing tools can support and where further work is needed.
- What the next step involves, who needs to be involved, and what still needs to be confirmed.

We review the architecture, trade-offs, and implementation plan together so you can decide the next step with complete clarity.

### A few practical questions

**How do you decide between custom software, automation, and AI?**

Each tool solves a different part of an operational bottleneck. Deterministic software and business rules handle pricing calculations, permissions, and data pipelines where every run must follow exact logic. AI handles unstructured documents, cross-system synthesis, and first-draft preparation for a person to review. Most engagements combine both around the systems your team already uses.

**Can you work with our existing team or technology provider?**

Yes. I can work alongside your team and existing providers. We agree on who is making decisions, doing the work, testing it, and supporting it afterward.

**How much does a Discovery Sprint cost?**

I'll propose a fee once we've agreed on the question and scope. You'll know the fee, timing, and what's included before deciding to proceed. Any implementation is scoped and priced separately.

## MTRO PRO story

### Product repairs, automated browser QA, and a daily seven-source account brief.

MTRO PRO

External link: Visit MTRO PRO → https://mtropro.com/

I joined MTRO PRO, a rental software business, in early 2026 as an engineer and technical advisor working across customer onboarding and product delivery. I worked directly with rental operators during setup, traced where onboarding stalled in the application, and shipped both product fixes and internal tooling for the team.

### A seven-source account brief, ready every morning.

Before following up with an operator, I needed to see where setup stood and what had been agreed in the last conversation. A product event showed whether payments were connected, while a message or booked call explained the next step. Assembling that context across accounts every morning was a clear candidate for automation.

I built a daily pipeline that pulls from seven sources to generate an account brief and collects incoming help requests into a review queue. Staff review the account history and draft response before replying to the customer. Health checks and alerts flag missing data feeds immediately, distinguishing a quiet account from a broken integration.

### Product defects the developers could reproduce and retest.

Booking, lease signing, and payment have to work as a single connected journey. I built an AI-assisted browser testing workflow that steps through those flows, records the evidence, and gives developers the exact state needed to reproduce a failure. Every repair runs through the same journey before release.

More than a dozen test rounds ran between March and April. One booking review uncovered seven defects across lease signing, checkout, and payment status. Written test plans, browser captures, and automated retesting gave the team a reliable way to prioritize fixes and verify each release.

### Property imports restored, with instructions for the team.

When an upstream login change broke property imports, I reproduced the failure, shipped and verified an interim repair, and wrote setup documentation for the team. Another developer then migrated the integration to scoped API keys, and I prepared repository-level onboarding instructions for a new contractor joining the import work.

The account-research and support-intake systems have run in production since July. Every morning, the team starts with current account context and queued support drafts, leaving staff to decide how to respond, which defects to prioritize, and when to release.

Custom manufacturing

**From a complex quoting workbook to company-owned software and fractional CTO leadership.**

Card: A systems assessment for a custom manufacturer grew into an ongoing fractional CTO role on the management team. The work spans a custom quoting and intake application, management reporting tied directly to ERP source records, and moving critical infrastructure into company custody.

The owner of a growing custom manufacturer brought me in for a systems assessment as the business outgrew its spreadsheets and outside contractors. He needed a technical counterpart to evaluate engineering work, identify where existing tools were holding the operation back, and set a practical roadmap. That assessment grew into an ongoing fractional CTO role on the management team, building custom software for quoting and intake, tying management reporting directly to ERP records, and bringing critical infrastructure under company ownership.

**A custom quoting application built around the team's pricing logic.** Customer quotes were running through a sprawling spreadsheet workbook where pricing logic, material yields, labor, and quantity breaks were tightly coupled. Comparing quantity options or revising an estimate meant duplicating workbooks by hand and re-checking formulas across sheets. I worked with the estimating and design team to translate that workbook logic and its material lookup tables into a custom web application the company owns. An estimator or salesperson enters the product type, dimensions, materials, and target quantities. The system calculates material yield, setup, labor, outside components, and freight across quantity options in one pass, keeps internal cost breakdowns alongside customer pricing, tracks revisions side by side, and generates a customer-ready PDF.

**Management reporting tied directly to ERP source transactions.** Because the ERP's built-in reports were difficult to use directly, management reporting relied on multi-sheet workbooks that were hard to reconcile when leadership wanted to inspect a number. Using on-time delivery and customer revenue views as the starting point, I traced the reported figures back to raw ERP source transactions. That audit resolved mismatches where order lines were grouped differently than full orders, partial periods were compared against full months, customer names varied in formatting, or an automated data feed had stalled. I worked with the owner to define explicit business rules for how orders, dates, and customer records are counted, built the reporting pipeline in a cloud data warehouse, and equipped an internal specialist with direct warehouse access and a verification process to maintain reporting in-house.

**Structured intake and handoffs between sales, design, and estimating.** Quoting is only part of the workflow; custom jobs also stall when requests move between sales, design, sample production, and estimating without complete specifications or clear ownership. I worked with leadership and department leads to design an intake and routing system that connects the CRM, engineering design briefs, and the quoting application. The workflow separates incoming work into dedicated operational queues for sales requests, house accounts, engineering, drawings, and samples, carrying customer records, drawings, and specifications together so each person receives a complete brief and a clear next task.

**Fractional CTO leadership across infrastructure custody, hiring, and vendor decisions.** Joining the management team gave the owner an ongoing technical counterpart across operations and engineering. That role resolves day-to-day technical risk alongside new builds: moving automations and cloud infrastructure off outside contractors' accounts into company custody and version control, putting release checks around internal tools so updates do not disrupt daily work, leading technical hiring evaluations and developer transitions, and reviewing software vendor proposals with the owner before the company commits capital.

What these systems resolve across the operation:

- **Multi-quantity quoting and revisions:** Translates spreadsheet pricing logic and material lookup tables into a custom web application that prices quantity options together, preserves version history, and generates customer-ready PDFs.
- **ERP-backed management reporting:** Connects management reporting directly to raw ERP transactions in a cloud data warehouse with explicit business definitions, resolving spreadsheet discrepancies and enabling in-house maintenance.
- **Connected intake and engineering handoffs:** Links customer context, specifications, and drawings across dedicated queues for sales requests, house accounts, engineering, drawings, and samples so work moves with a complete brief.
- **Company-owned infrastructure and technical governance:** Brings cloud accounts, automations, and source code into company custody and version control, while supporting developer transitions and vendor evaluations.

**Technical leadership focused on company-owned systems.** Across quoting, intake handoffs, ERP reporting, and infrastructure custody, the work replaces disconnected spreadsheets and contractor dependencies with clear, maintainable systems the company owns.

How to measure the benefit

Measure the business impact through employee effort and elapsed time separately. Compare similar jobs before and after, using the same start and end events. Split repeat work from new designs so a change in job mix does not look like an improvement.

Outcome Measure

Employee time Hands-on minutes spent entering information, estimating, preparing documents, and handling revisions per request.

Approval time Time from request received to approved design. Track the median and slower cases; separate time working from time waiting.

Avoidable rework Repeat effort caused by missing information, an old file, or an incorrect handoff. Keep genuine customer scope changes separate.

Capacity Comparable requests completed per person, alongside backlog age and quality. Faster processing should not move the problem downstream.

For labor savings, multiply the observed reduction in minutes per request by comparable monthly request volume, then divide by 60. Add a labor cost only after the time difference has been measured.
