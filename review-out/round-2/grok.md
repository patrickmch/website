# Round 2 review

Screenshots in `screenshots/tiles/` and `public/og.png` were read, along with `docs/design-spec.md`, `docs/website-copy-2026-10.md`, and the source listed in the brief.

## Verdict

I would not ship this. The sentences, the type size, the contact form, and the seated photograph are worth publishing. The visual system built around them is not. In the first screen an owner gets a tracked-caps label, the headline "Make it easier to take on more business.", a second paragraph that offers "AI tools," and an orange button, twice, with the right half of the desktop page empty. The diagrams that were supposed to show the work arrive as gray wireframes, and one of them names Maria at Hillside. The captions say "Illustrative." The published page then shows no quote, no sample, and no fee. The single biggest risk is that those pictures are taken as fake proof, and that Patrick is still several screens down when the owner decides.

## What works

- Headlines at 36–56px, weight 500, in a column near 65 characters. The pages read at the size of a letter.
- The four problem sentences are the strongest copy on the site, and setting each as a heading over its paragraph gives them room.
- The seated portrait is sharp, unfiltered, and obviously one person, in the navy shirt the alt text describes.
- The contact form has labels above the fields, no slogan placeholders, the copy's exact error sentences, and a full-width button on the phone.
- "A few practical questions" is open on the page. "Does it have to be an AI project?" is answered there in plain sentences, which is where that topic belongs.
- Unfilled proof slots render nothing in the published tree. No fake quotation is presented as a client's words.
- Fig. 6's four endings, "Take it forward with your team," "Use another provider," "Ask me to scope the next stage," and "Stop here," are the right content. The drawing fails them. The content should survive.
- The skip link, `:focus-visible` outlines, real paths, the old `#/` rewrite, and a contrast table that was actually measured are serious front-end work. Keep that bar.
- The paper `#F4F2ED` and the ink `#16202B` are calm, and they are a real departure from the terracotta coaching palette. The color that needs to change is the use of the orange, not the paper.

## Alternative directions

**A letter.** Set the existing copy in the prose column, in the serif and the sans already chosen, with hairline section rules and no cards, no figure numbers, no ink banner, and no diagrams. Move the seated photograph up to sit beside "You'll work directly with me," and make the only orange control the button that sends the form. The four problems, the sprint explanation, the "you can stop there" paragraph, and the FAQ are already the sales argument. This is the register the contact page has now, extended to the other three pages. An owner who distrusts agency sites will trust it sooner, and it can ship before a testimonial exists, because it does not pretend to display anyone's operation. The cost is that the site will not look like a designed object in a portfolio. For this reader, that cost is a benefit.

**One findings document, shown.** The concept is right at the scale of a single artifact and wrong at the scale of six schematic widgets. The middle of Home, or the middle of Working Together, should be one redacted Discovery Sprint deliverable a visitor can read without signing up: a quote or a job packet, the step where it waited marked with the pen, the recommendation, and the four ways it can end. That is the thing the ink block is advertising. Fig. 4's missing PO and Fig. 6's four endings are the only diagram ideas worth keeping, and they belong on that document, not in a row of components. A clearly redacted real packet beats a clean fictional one. If a real packet cannot be shown, this direction waits, and direction one is the site in the meantime.

**Photographs of the work, or no pictures of the work.** The spec's own later note, a paper quote with pen notes, a whiteboard, a stack of job folders, shot by Patrick at a real site, is the version of "draw the work" an owner would believe. Those photographs have not been taken. CSS boxes with mono labels are a substitute, and they look like the software pitch. Until the photographs exist, publish the letter and do not draw a job board. A ridge portrait and a process diagram are both pictures of something other than the reader's operation.
