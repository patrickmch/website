A connected application for custom manufacturing

One application. A connected path from request to production.

I built a custom application for a custom manufacturer to connect customer intake, estimating and reporting around its existing systems. The larger vision is a shared record that follows each job from the first request through design, quoting and the production handoff. People keep their specialist tools; the application carries the context between them.

The operating problem: a request could involve sales, estimating, engineering and production, and each needed different information about the same job. The company already had an ERP, CRM and workflow tools. Too much of the connection between them depended on spreadsheets, attachments and people remembering what had changed.

The information existed. People had to put it back together.

Sales collected the customer’s request. Estimating needed dimensions, materials, quantities and buying context. Engineering needed the specification and the reason for a change. Management needed a dependable view of the work. The handoffs between those needs were where information was re-entered, lost or discovered missing.

  • Re-enter the same facts

    Dimensions, material and quantities move from the request to the estimate, then into the order.

  • Reconstruct the change

    A revised requirement means checking the drawing, recalculating the price and finding who is waiting.

  • Chase the current answer

    The latest file, the agreed specification and the status can live in different places.

One place to move the work forward.

The organizing idea is a shared job record. Intake starts it before an ERP part number exists. Estimating adds prices. Engineering adds its decision and revision. The accepted work is prepared for the ERP. Each person sees the same job through the view they need.

The ERP is the company’s official record of parts, orders and transactions. The custom app handles the work around those records: gathering requirements, resolving questions, comparing options and getting the right information to the next person. Engineering retains control of technical approval.

  • The foundation I’ve built

    Saved intake, quoting with the company’s estimating rules, quantity alternatives, version comparison and customer and internal outputs. Receiving handoffs have been exercised in controlled tests. The reporting module is built and source-validated locally.

  • The larger connected workflow

    Carry that context through one shared work-in-progress record, connect design revisions and review status, and return confirmed ERP references to the job. The next integrations and team rollout are staged around real work.

What I would build around custom gasket work.

Your engineering team helps customers work through drawings, material requirements and compatibility. I would start by following one of those requests with your team, then build the application around how it moves from a requirement to an approved, quoted item.

Then the customer changes the material.

In this design, that change would open a new version and flag the affected design and quote for review. The technical team confirms suitability, estimating revises the price, and the approved version becomes the basis of the production handoff. The earlier version stays in the history.

That is the connection I would build: the change, the people who need to act on it and the work it affects remain attached to the same request.

Based on your public engineering and design services. This is a proposed starting point, not an assessment of your current internal process.

Inside the application

These are the implemented building blocks behind that vision. They handle incomplete requirements, different estimating routes, changing quantities, saved prices and information intended for different audiences.

01

Intake: give the next person a usable brief.

The request is saved as structured information, with its source context and files, rather than existing only as an attachment someone has to interpret again.

  • Keep customer and item context together. The request retains the selected customer, item information, quantities and supporting documents.
  • Represent the work being requested. A request can carry quoting, drawing, engineering or sample needs. Those are different kinds of work, even when they concern the same item.
  • Review the handoff before submitting. The operator can inspect the proposed destination, request brief and files. A saved request can be found and reopened without starting the brief again.
  • Keep a revision tied to its source. Changes create saved versions, so the submitted information can be distinguished from subsequent edits.

Why the detail matters

An order quantity, the number of components in a set and a sample quantity mean different things. The intake work was designed around preserving those distinctions, along with the difference between “quote this item” and “help design a solution.”

The operator still has to confirm the requirement. A file attached to a request is not, by itself, an approved design.

02

Quoting: put the company’s estimating logic into the application.

The quote tool translates workbook-based estimating into a web workflow. It keeps the commercial calculation, manufacturing information and customer presentation connected to a saved quote.

  • Calculate the supported construction. Inputs include product style, dimensions, material and quantity. The calculation covers the relevant sheet layout and yield, material, labor and pricing rules for the selected route.
  • Compare buying quantities. Quantity alternatives are treated as alternatives for the customer to choose between, rather than quantities to add into one order total.
  • Inspect the economics internally. Internal views expose cost, freight, margin, unit price and extended price where applicable. The customer output presents the commercial quote without private estimating notes or internal economics.
  • Carry the manufacturing details forward. Internal manufacturing and part-preparation views retain the specifications associated with the quoted work. ERP creation remains a separately controlled step.
  • Save the whole quote. Inputs, prices, lines and context stay with the saved version. The operator can find a quote, reopen it and produce the customer-facing PDF from that version.

A practical distinction

A customer asking for three quantity breaks needs three comparable buying options. Estimating needs to see what each option costs to make. The customer needs the offered prices.

Those are two views of the same quote. The application separates the presentation without losing the underlying calculation.

Supported product families and manufacturing routes are implemented and validated individually. The tool does not silently substitute one construction for another or treat a prepared ERP record as a completed ERP transaction.

03

Revisions: make the saved version the reference.

A change should not erase the basis of the earlier quote or leave the receiver guessing which information was sent.

  • Preserve earlier versions. A revised quote creates a successor rather than silently rewriting the previous saved result.
  • Compare what changed. The quote comparison view shows changes between saved versions, including added, removed or changed lines.
  • Keep outputs aligned. Customer PDFs and internal views are produced from the selected saved version. Internal notes remain separate from the customer document.
  • Package the handoff. The receiving flow prepares a brief, summary and original supporting file. Controlled verification checked the actual received package, not just whether the sending screen reported success.

How this relates to revision work

The avoidable work is reconstructing a requirement, re-entering specifications or repeating a review because the supporting information is unclear.

The application preserves that context. Engineering still decides whether a changed material, dimension or drawing is technically suitable.

04

Reporting: bring actuals, plans and activity into a usable view.

The reporting module connects the company’s existing reporting data and working spreadsheets. It is built around the questions the team already tracks, with the underlying numbers checked against their sources.

  • Separate different kinds of growth. Total, existing-customer and new-customer growth views distinguish progress with existing customers from new-customer sales.
  • Look beneath the headline. Rep scoreboards include customer detail, prior-year comparisons, bookings and goals. Users can filter by period, search customers and export filtered data.
  • Connect activity to the operating view. Separate tabs cover meeting activity and prospect lists alongside the sales scoreboards.
  • Keep the source and reporting period explicit. Actuals and bookings come from the existing reporting data source. Plans, meeting activity and prospect inputs come from working sheets. Completed-month and partial-month reporting are labeled rather than blended invisibly.

Built, then checked against the source

The reporting validation compared 3,304 rep, growth, target and meeting-count values with their source records. It also exercised customer drilldown and CSV export.

This module has been built and tested as a local presentation. It is not represented here as a completed company-wide dashboard migration.

What changes for the people doing the work

The business value comes from removing steps around the work: assembling a brief, repeating a calculation, finding a previous version or rebuilding a report.

  • Sales

    A saved request carries the customer’s context, the item and the supporting files. The next person has a brief to review instead of another disconnected spreadsheet.

  • Estimating

    The company’s supported calculations, quantity options and internal economics live in the quoting workflow. A changed request can become a new version of the existing quote.

  • Engineering and receiving teams

    The handoff identifies the requested work and carries the selected information and original files. Technical review remains with the people qualified to make it.

  • Management

    The reporting module brings source data into comparable periods and drilldowns. A headline number can be followed back to the customer or activity behind it.

Built from the way the company actually works

The job was to understand and implement the operating logic, not just reproduce the appearance of the spreadsheets.

  • 01

    Start with real work

    Map how requests reach sales, estimating and engineering. Inspect the workbooks, source records and handoffs the team relies on. Identify where information is duplicated or arrives too late.

  • 02

    Make the rules explicit

    Translate the existing calculations into defined behavior. Preserve quantities, units, pricing versions and output differences. Keep unresolved business decisions visible rather than hiding them in a formula.

  • 03

    Verify the whole handoff

    Check calculation results against source examples, reopen saved work, inspect the actual customer PDF and verify received files. For reporting, reconcile the displayed values with the source.

Implemented software, a verified integration and broad team adoption are different milestones. The intake and quoting capabilities described here are implemented; controlled receiving and the local reporting module have the narrower rollout scopes stated above.
